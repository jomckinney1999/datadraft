# DataDraft

**LeetCode for football data.** A question bank over real NFL scoring, courses that build up to it, and projects that end in something a learner can show someone. Next.js 14 (App Router), TypeScript, Tailwind, deployed to Vercel at https://data-draft.vercel.app (the old `sql-sports.vercel.app` 307s there). Source is public at https://github.com/jomckinney1999/datadraft. Both URLs live once, in `lib/site.ts`.

## What this is, and what it deliberately is not

The product narrowed on 2026-09-30 after a round of outside feedback. **Four sections, and that is the whole site:** Dashboard · Questions · Courses · Projects.

- **The bet is retention, not completion.** A course is a thing you finish once; a daily question is a thing you come back to. When a change would make the site better at being finished but worse at being returned to, it is the wrong change.
- **Gone on purpose, and not to be re-added without a conversation:** career pathways and the job-title picker (`lib/career-paths.ts`, the `/learn/path/*` board), the career kit at `/resources` (resume outlines, outreach cadence, book shelf), the two-question course chooser at `/learn/start`, the Udemy-style video Studio, and the Draft Day ceremony. Career coaching is a crowded shelf we have no edge on, and every hour spent there was an hour not spent on questions.
- **Football only.** `lib/sports.ts`, `lib/use-sport.ts` and the sport picker are deleted. Basketball and baseball were selectable and changed nothing — every lesson, question and sandbox is NFL data. The `sport` column in Supabase survives and is written as the constant `"football"`, so a second sport stays a migration rather than a schema change.
- **`next.config.mjs` redirects every retired route** to the thing that replaced it (`/interview` → `/projects`, `/learn/project/:id` → `/projects/:id`, the career surfaces → `/learn`). Keep it that way — those URLs are in people's history and in search results. `/courses` redirects to `/learn`, which is where the catalogue has always lived; the nav calls it Courses.

## Design system

Fantasy-football palette, defined once in `tailwind.config.ts` + `app/globals.css`: Duolingo-neutral charcoal canvas (`#131F24`) with premium accents — turf green `#58CC02`, ice blue `#1CB0F6`, gold `#FFC800`. Structural gray from the charcoal family. An older teal/amber scheme (and a brief pure-black Engineered Energy pass) were replaced — never reintroduce `teal`/`amber` token names. Change UI colors by editing token values, not component classes.

- **The canvas is charcoal on purpose** — green/blue/gold pop against a neutral dark field without washing toward navy or cream.
- **`ice` is the blue accent** (broadcast cool / secondary). **Success and reward stay turf green.** Gold is milestones and prestige.
- **`node scripts/verify-contrast.mjs` measures the shipped tokens, 48 pairings.** Run it after any palette edit. It reads the real values out of `globals.css` rather than a copy.

### Depth, light and material

The old palette was flat by construction: panels sat a few RGB points above the page, borders were 1px solid, and nothing cast or caught light. These give the same sharp broadcast-terminal identity somewhere to sit.

- **`.surface` is the card treatment** — a top-down lightening across the fill, a 1px specular line on the top edge, and a shadow that reads as distance. Add it alongside the existing `border border-panel-border bg-panel` pattern; it is on ~47 cards. Light mode zeroes the first two (a highlight needs somewhere darker than itself to sit) and keeps the shadow.
- **The new material classes live in `@layer components`, and that placement is load-bearing.** Unlayered, `.surface` would beat `shadow-scoreboard-gold` and strip the gold ring off the draft announcement, and `.chrome` would turn `absolute` frames into `relative` ones. In the components layer a Tailwind utility always wins, which is the right default for a component-level treatment.
- **`shadow-scoreboard` and `.surface` are mutually exclusive.** The former is a 1px ring in the same colour as the border the card already has — pure duplication — and being a utility it wins, silently costing the card its elevation. It was removed from the eight cards that gained `.surface`; the `-gold`/`-turf` variants are deliberate accent rings and stay.
- **`.bg-stadium` sits on `<body>`, so its atmosphere is site-wide.** Three coloured washes plus film grain, both on `position: fixed` pseudo-elements at `z-index: -2`/`-1` — the page scrolls *through* the light instead of dragging a gradient with it, and no component has to know they exist. The grain is not decoration: large smooth gradients on a near-black canvas band visibly on 8-bit displays, and a few percent of noise dithers the steps away.
- **`.btn-turf` / `.btn-gold`** are the primary-CTA treatment (gradient fill, specular top edge, light spilling underneath). The spill multiplies by `--glow-strength`, so it vanishes in light mode rather than becoming a coloured smear.
- **`.chrome`** is the gradient hairline (turf → ice → gold) on the hero sandbox frame, done as a masked border rather than four elements.
- **The photo duotone is held at ~58% alpha.** `mix-blend-mode: color` takes the overlay's hue *and* saturation wholesale, so at full opacity the accents turn `components/action-gallery.tsx` into a poster. Re-check that treatment if the accents ever gain more chroma again.
- **Focus rings, selection colour and scrollbars are themed once, globally.** A default scrollbar is a light-grey slab bolted onto a dark page and is one of the loudest tells that a dark theme was applied rather than designed.

### Dark only (no light mode)

One theme: Duolingo-neutral charcoal (`#131F24`) with green / blue / gold accents. Tokens are `"r g b"` channel triplets in `app/globals.css` under `:root`, wired into Tailwind as `rgb(var(--c-x) / <alpha-value>)` so alpha modifiers (`bg-turf/10`) keep working.

- **Use the tokens.** Never hardcode a hex, `rgba()`, or `text-[#...]` in a component. For glows/scrims/gradients, add a class in `globals.css` (see `.rays-turf`, `.yard-lines`, `.edge-glow-gold`) rather than an inline `style`. The illustrated cast — Coach Blitz (`components/coach.tsx`) and the Rookie and Ref (`components/sideline-cast.tsx`) — is the deliberate exception: illustration hexes stay fixed.
- **Accents:** turf green `#58CC02`, ice blue `#1CB0F6`, gold `#FFC800`. All text/accent pairings clear WCAG AA — `node scripts/verify-contrast.mjs` after any palette edit.
- **No theme toggle.** `ThemeToggle` is a no-op; `app/layout.tsx` forces `data-theme="dark"` and clears any leftover `sqlsports-theme` preference.
- **Testing resets:** `components/testing-tools.tsx` on course roadmaps (`/learn/track/...`) — restart or complete a course, refill timeouts, or wipe all local progress. Also usable from `/demo`. It writes the same localStorage the real player reads, deliberately: a mock mode would let a bug live in the real path while the test surface stayed green.

## Questions (`/questions`) — the centre of the product

`lib/questions.ts` is a bank of standalone problems in **SQL, Python, R and Excel** over the same real data the lessons use. A question is a situation, what you are working on, and a result you have to produce. No drive, no down, no timeout spent — a wrong answer costs the twenty seconds you spent on it, and that cheapness is the feature.

- **Four runtimes, one surface** (`components/question-workspace.tsx`). SQL runs sql.js against the pinned lesson database and is graded on the result grid. Python and R run in Pyodide / WebR (`lib/runtimes.ts`) against a `setup` prelude and are graded on **printed output**. Excel evaluates a real formula against `lib/excel-data.ts` and is graded on the **value produced**, with the same anti-hardcode guard as the lessons.
- **`PY_SETUP` / `R_SETUP` are the shared preludes** — the same twenty players and real 2024 totals the SQL questions aggregate, read out of the database rather than typed from memory. Both the learner's code and the answer key run against the identical prelude, so grading compares two programs that saw the same world. A Python or R question without a `setup` fails the verifier.
- **SQL and Excel load instantly; Python is ~12 MB and R ~30 MB.** Those two are fetched only when someone opens a question in that language and presses something — never on page load — and the button says what it is about to cost. **The landing page and the dashboard are pinned to the SQL daily for exactly this reason.**

- **Grading compares result grids, not query text** (`lib/sql-grade.ts`), so a CTE and a subquery both pass. That makes the **`returns` field load-bearing rather than decorative**: if the prompt does not say which columns in which order, a learner can have the right idea and still fail on a column they were never asked for. The verifier fails a question with an empty `returns`.
- **Question of the Day, one per language.** A single rotation over the whole bank would hand a SQL learner an R question on a Tuesday and break a streak they had done nothing to lose, so each language has its own; `isDailyQuestion(day, q)` says whether a question is its language's daily, and solving any of them advances the same streak. `questionOfTheDay(day, lang)` picks from **`leagueDay()` — America/New_York**, not the viewer's clock. Two reasons, both load-bearing: everyone worldwide gets the same problem, so it is something two people can argue about; and a server-rendered date agrees with the client's instead of tearing hydration. The day is always resolved in a **server** component and passed down as a prop — never recomputed in the browser.
- **The rotation walks a stride co-prime with each language's pool size**, so every question gets its day before any repeats. Add a question and the stride recomputes; the verifier fails if the first N days in any language contain fewer than N distinct questions, and fails outright if a language the filter offers has nothing behind it.
- **XP pays once per question; tickets pay every time.** Grinding a solved question must not inflate the number that is supposed to mean progress, but coming back should still be worth something. `progress.solvedQuestions` is the record; `qotdStreak` / `qotdLastDay` are kept apart from the lesson `streak` because "you showed up" and "you solved the day's problem" are different claims.
- **Run `node scripts/verify-answer-keys.mjs` after touching any question.** SQL keys run against the real seeded database, Python keys in Pyodide, Excel keys through the shipped engine. It fails on: a key that errors, returns no rows, prints nothing, returns blank, has no `FROM` (a hardcoded answer), references no cell, sits on a tie at a `LIMIT` cutoff, names a table or sheet that does not exist, has a duplicate id, or has no `returns` line. **R keys are skipped** — there is no supported Node build of WebR — and the script says so rather than passing them silently, which is why the R questions stay deliberately short and obviously-right-on-reading. It has already caught a real one: an XLOOKUP key searching for a player who was not on that sheet.
- **The day's question is a hero, and it shows faces** (`components/qotd-card.tsx`, on `/questions`, the dashboard in `compact`, and in the landing panel and workspace banner via `FaceCluster`). The faces are the players the question is *about*, from `lib/question-players.ts`: `question.players` where the author named someone, else the top players at a position the prompt mentions, else a stable slice of the roster keyed on the question id. **Never random** — a card that reshuffles on re-render flickers, and a server render that disagrees with the client tears hydration. Headshots are baked by `scripts/build-player-headshots.mjs` into `lib/player-headshots.generated.ts` (newest season first, so a player who moved shows his current team), hotlinked from the NFL CDN as nflverse lists them, never proxied. **The URL nflverse lists is the 3400x2450 master, about 4 MB per face.** The CDN is Cloudinary-shaped and honours added transforms, so headshotSrc() in components/headshot.tsx requests a face-detected square (c_thumb,g_face) at twice the rendered size, about 25 KB. Never render a raw headshot_url. The verifier fails a `players` name that is not one of the twenty lesson players.
- **Workspace quality-of-life, all deliberate:** ⌘/Ctrl+Enter submits (caught on a wrapper around `CodeEditor`, since the keydown bubbles out of the textarea). A wrong answer gets one sentence about its *shape* — column names and row count for SQL, line count for printed output, kind of value for Excel — never values, which is the difference between a hint and a leak. On phones the editor sits directly under the prompt and the tables/hints come after; on desktop the editor spans both rows on the right (`lg:row-span-2`). Prev/next stay inside the language. The language filter on `/questions` is remembered per browser (`sqlsports.questions.lang`) so a Python learner lands on the Python daily; picking All clears it.
- **Card art is `components/question-art.tsx`, and the picture is what the title literally says.** "Name the Quarterbacks" is a quarterback mid-throw, "Thirty Burger" a burger with a 30 flag, "The Drop-Off" a ball going off a cliff — 43 scenes for 46 questions (three title ideas are shared). It used to be eight generic scenes rotated across the bank, so a quarterback question wore a clipboard; **a new question that no scene fits gets a new scene, not the nearest one** — borrowing is how it drifted. Drawn, not photographed (licensing, as with the covers); figures wear a position, never a number; every colour is a token; anything computed with trig is rounded to one decimal so server and client print identical attributes.
- **The daily Stat Duel** (`/questions/duel`; `lib/stat-duel.ts`, `components/stat-duel.tsx`, `components/duel-card.tsx`; decided 2026-10-02) is the no-code way in. Five head-to-heads on real numbers ("Who scored more PPR points in the 2023 season: Justin Jefferson or Puka Nacua?"). You tap one and both numbers appear. "Prove it" shows the SQL behind the round, and "Run it yourself" loads the lesson database and runs it, editable, with Chart it. At the end: a score, a 🟩🟥 grid to share, a days-in-a-row count, and a link to today's SQL question. Linked from the questions page, the dashboard and the home page's today's-question section, never from the nav. Things to keep true:
  - **Trivia about the data, never football lore.** Every number is computed from the pinned lesson rows and `games`, and every round carries the SQL that produces it. `verify-answer-keys.mjs` generates 180 days from launch and fails if a shown number disagrees with its SQL, if a round ties, if two positions are matched against each other, if the SQL's first row isn't the answer, if a day can't fill five rounds, or if a day comes out differently twice.
  - **Same five for everyone,** seeded from `leagueDay()` in the server component, never `Math.random()`. Rounds get closer as the day goes on (bands of the pair list sorted by gap), names don't repeat within a day, and the answer side is seeded too (about half land on each side).
  - **Templates:** season total, points per game, best single game, 20-point games, one week head-to-head, team points scored, defence points allowed. Averages sitting exactly on a rounding half are left out, so JavaScript and SQLite can't disagree on the display. New templates need the same SQL-backed value and a verifier pass.
  - **No betting language:** "who had more", never "over/under".
  - Progress is per browser: `sqlsports.duel.v1` (today's picks, so a reload resumes or reopens on the score) and `sqlsports.duel.streak.v1`. Keys: ←/1 and →/2 pick, Enter continues (a focused button handles its own Enter, or a round would be skipped).
- **"Chart it" turns any SQL result into a chart worth posting** (decided 2026-10-02: the avatar is the fantasy league's numbers person, and charts are what fantasy Twitter shares). `components/chart-it.tsx` is a button beside the result on every SQL question, on the Practice Field, and in the League Scorecard's league panel. It opens a dialog: bars / scatter / lines, column pickers, an editable title and subtitle, editable corner captions on a scatter, then Download PNG, Copy image, Share (where the browser shares files) and Post on X (an intent link back to the page, `?ref=chart`). Things to keep true:
  - **The preview is a canvas, and it is the PNG.** `lib/chart-draw.ts` paints 1600×900 (16:9, the shape X shows uncropped) with the page's own fonts. SVG was ruled out because an SVG turned into an image can't reach web fonts, so the export would have come out in system type.
  - **What a result can be is decided in `lib/chart.ts`, pure and DOM-free:** which columns are measurements (numbers that aren't ids, weeks or seasons), which hold team abbreviations, and the suggested kind. Two or more measurements give a scatter; a week or season plus a measurement, where each name has a run of weeks, gives lines; anything else gives ranked bars. Nothing to chart (under two rows, no numbers) means no button.
  - **The look is the sticker art at chart scale:** night-outlined dots and bars, labels with a night halo, dashed averages labelled like the charts it imitates, team colours via `teamAccent`, the leading bar in gold, the wordmark and avatar bottom-left. **Labels never collide.** They are placed extremes-first, try snug spots and then a ring farther out with a leader line, keep 6px of air, and are left off rather than overprinted. A repeated name gains its week, or else its team, so a traded player isn't two dots both called "Davante Adams". With corner captions and no team colours, dots take their corner's colour.
  - **Every image carries the credit.** nflverse data is CC BY 4.0, so `NFLVERSE_CREDIT` is mandatory on question and Practice Field charts. A league chart says it's the learner's own league, via Sleeper.
- **The League Scorecard runs in the browser too** (`components/league-lab.tsx`, `lib/league-load.ts`, on `/projects/my-league-scorecard`). A Sleeper username (the API sends `access-control-allow-origin: *`, so it's fetched straight from the visitor's tab and never touches our servers) or the notebook's ESPN/Yahoo CSV loads into sql.js as **the notebook's exact tables**: `managers`, `weekly_scores`, `starter_points`, same columns and order, so a query moves between the page and Colab unchanged. Five presets, each with its own chart: "Who's actually good?" (all-play vs real win %, corners Lucky / Legit / Bad / Unlucky), the schedule scatter, total points, points left on the bench, and the points race (a running-total window function, because twelve lines of raw weekly scores is spaghetti). **Only completed regular-season weeks count**, on the page and in the notebook alike: a week in progress or a future week comes back from Sleeper as zero points and would read as a 0–0 game, and playoffs skew all-play. Checked against Sleeper's documented sample league: page and notebook rules give identical numbers. That group chat is the growth loop: ten or twelve fantasy players looking at a chart of their own league with the site's name on it.
- **The landing page's `components/qotd-panel.tsx` is the same question, playable without an account** — no XP, no streak, no progress write. It replaced a free-form SQL sandbox that handed visitors a blinking cursor and an empty database and asked them to think of something to ask it. Most people typed nothing.

### The landing page's motion

- **The page talks to one person** (decided 2026-10-02; the avatar, the messaging and the $20k/month math are in `docs/PLAN.md`): the fantasy league's numbers person who wants it to be their job. Headline: "You're already the numbers person in your league. / **Make it your job** ▢". The two asks, in the hero and the final CTA, are **Solve today's question** (links straight to today's SQL daily, with "Free · no signup · 90 seconds" under it) and **Chart your league** (`/projects/my-league-scorecard#your-league`). The ticker names that person four ways instead of four audiences. Section headings are in their words ("Like waivers, but for your career", "Your league, in your portfolio"), the FAQ opens with their objections (will employers take it seriously, I only know Excel, how much time, my league isn't on Sleeper), and "you don't have to watch the games" lives in the FAQ, not the hero. New copy should keep sounding like that, and never promise a job.
- **`components/chart-your-league.tsx` is the second section**, straight after today's question: the league panel's pitch beside a real example chart (`public/charts/nfl-luck-2025.png`, made with Chart it from the `games` table). It's real NFL teams, not an invented league, because every number here is real and a real league would put strangers' names on the front page. It's point differential vs. win % (Lucky / Legit / Bad / Unlucky), not all-play, because in the NFL points against is defence, not luck, and an all-play chart would call good defences lucky. Re-make it from any SQL question page if the chart look changes.
- **The hero is exactly one screen** (`min-h-[calc(100svh-3.5rem-1px)]`, the nav is 57px), content centred, in the shape of analystbuilder.com's front door; the "built for" ticker and the six stat cards start where the scroll starts, and a bobbing Scroll cue links to them. `svh` not `vh`, so a phone's collapsing address bar never pushes the buttons off.
- **The home page opens on a title screen** (`components/title-gate.tsx` + `title-gate-client.tsx`, `components/referee.tsx`, `lib/whistle.ts`, `.title-gate` / `.gate-*` / `.ref-*` in globals.css; decided 2026-09-30). The DataDraft wordmark, a big outlined referee bobbing in front of turning rays, and a gold "Press any button" sticker ("Tap anywhere" on touch screens). Any key or tap blows his whistle — pose snap, puffed cheeks, "TWEEET!" starburst, shockwave rings, shake — and a gold-rimmed cartoon iris opens onto the site while the hero springs up. Things to keep true:
  - **The press is what makes the sound possible.** Browsers refuse audio until the visitor has pressed or tapped something, so a whistle "on load" can't reliably play. The gate is that press. `blowWhistle()` must be called inside the key or tap handler.
  - **The whistle is synthesised, not a file**: a ~2.9 kHz note with a ~30 Hz "pea" warbling pitch and level, plus band-passed breath, played as a short then a long blast. Measured on the live audio path: peak ≈ 0.19 (no clipping), dominant ≈ 2.9–2.97 kHz. The CSS delays the burst, shake and second ring by 0.25s to land on the long blast (`WHISTLE.longAt`); change one, change the other.
  - **Once per browser session, home page only, never for crawlers** (`sqlsports.gate.v1` in sessionStorage). It is an entrance, not a toll: the retention bet means a returning visitor shouldn't pay it twice in one visit.
  - **It decides before the first paint and can never lock anyone out.** An inline script sets `data-gate="on"` on `<html>` while the HTML parses, so there's no flash of hero. Without JS the gate never exists. If JS runs but React never hydrates, the script takes the gate back down after 8s. A press before hydration is remembered and played on mount.
  - **Poses are swapped, not tweened.** Both poses are always in the SVG, and `data-phase` (idle / blow / exit) picks one. That's how a cartoon does it — a held pose, a snap, a squash.
  - Reduced motion keeps the gate and the whistle (a key press and a sound aren't motion) but drops the bob, spin, shake, squash, rings, iris and hero spring; the burst just appears and the gate fades.
  - **On phones the gate is simpler, on purpose** (commit 3139d34): mobile Safari stalls on the screen-wide mask animation and turns the raised arm about the wrong point, flinging the ref off screen. So below 700px or on touch screens, the arm snaps instead of swinging, nothing shakes, the iris becomes a fade, the rays shrink, and the press button isn't auto-focused (focusing it scrolled the visual viewport under the fixed gate). Don't re-enable those for phones without testing on a real iPhone.
  - The in-app preview pane stops animating when its window is behind another, so a frozen gate there isn't a bug. Use Playwright to check the sequence.
- **The two front-door buttons are stickers** (`components/sticker-cta.tsx` + `.btn-sticker` in globals.css, hero and final CTA). The art is flat fills, the `night` outline and hard shadows; the buttons were mono capitals on a smooth gradient and looked like a different site. Now: the headline's display face in sentence case, a 3px night outline, a cel-shaded bottom band, a hard night "thickness" that grows on hover and goes to zero on press, a fading gloss pill, and an outlined sticker icon (a "?" bubble, the art kit's football) that wiggles on hover. `.btn-turf` / `.btn-gold` stay the compact in-app buttons; don't swap them for stickers everywhere, or the front door stops standing out.
- **The headline carries a tile that never stops turning over** (`components/headline-tile.tsx`, `.hl-*` in globals.css), after "Make it your job" on a line that never wraps (the tile belongs to the phrase, so the line steps down in size on phones rather than breaking). It cycles a football, a database, a Python snake, a spreadsheet fx, a rising chart and an R hex. Modelled on the icon analystbuilder.com flips inside its own headline — one small bright thing inside the biggest words is what makes a still hero feel alive. Six tiles share one 13.2s CSS loop, each offset by its 2.2s turn; no JavaScript. Holds on the football under reduced motion. (A football mouse cursor was tried on 2026-09-30 and removed the same day.)
- **`components/field-backdrop.tsx` + the `.field-*` / `.play-*` / `.ball-*` classes are the hero's moving field**, and it has to visibly move — the first version only drifted two floodlights over 26–34s and read as a still image. Layers: the turf plane *streams toward the camera* (a child inside the tilted plane translates one 320px pattern repeat and snaps back — translating keeps it on the compositor, animating background-position on a tilted masked layer would repaint every frame); **chalkboard plays** either side of the headline (X's and O's, routes that draw on with `pathLength="1"` dashes, hold, fade and redraw, staggered; hidden below `sm`); **two footballs thrown across the field** on long staggered loops (outer span crosses linearly, inner span rises and falls on its own easing and tilts with the arc); plus the floodlights, chalk dust and pointer glow. No canvas, no rAF loop except the pointer write. Under reduced motion the field holds still, the plays are drawn finished, and the footballs are removed.
- **The stat cards are `.glow-card`s inside `components/glow-grid.tsx`.** Each is a link to what it counts, with its own accent (`data-tone`): hover or focus lifts it, tints its border and the light under it, runs a bright arc round its edge (a conic gradient masked to a ring, turned by the registered `--glow-angle`, paused until hover), and a soft light follows the pointer (one listener on the grid writes `--mx/--my` onto the card under it). The reveal sits on a wrapper, not the card — `reveal-rise` holds its final transform with `both`, which would silently cancel the hover lift. The number glows with a `drop-shadow` filter and `--stat-tint`, not `text-shadow`: `.stat-glow` is gradient-clipped transparent text, unlayered, and a text-shadow would both lose to it and paint over the fill.
- **`components/course-rail.tsx` renders the catalogue twice and slides `-50%`**, so the second copy lands where the first began and the loop seam never shows. Width comes from the content, so adding a course needs no arithmetic. It pauses on hover *and* on focus-within — a card sliding out from under a cursor is an annoying way to lose a click, and a focused link that keeps moving is worse.
- **Scroll reveal: `components/reveal.tsx` + `.reveal` / `.sequence`.** Sections carry `data-reveal-section`; things inside carry `.reveal`, and `.sequence` staggers its children via `:nth-child` → `--reveal-index`, so a twelve-card row costs one observer on the section rather than twelve on the cards.
  - **Nothing hides until JavaScript arms it.** `.reveal` is only `opacity: 0` under `html[data-reveal-armed]`, which the component sets after mount. Without JS, before hydration, or if the observer never constructs, every `.reveal` is plain visible content. A reveal effect that can hide the whole page when a script fails is not an effect, it is an outage.
  - Anything already on screen at mount is revealed with `--reveal-duration: 0s`, because there was no scroll to trigger it and a hero that fades in after hydration reads as a flash. Sections are unobserved once revealed — scrolling back up does not replay it.
- **`components/workspace-shot.tsx` is a picture of the product, not the product.** Static server-rendered markup; the real workspace costs a WebAssembly database to boot. Everything in it — prompt, SQL, result rows — is lifted from the real `week-3-hammer` question, because a fabricated screenshot is the kind of small lie that gets found on the first click-through.
- **Every bit of this is off or reduced under `prefers-reduced-motion`**, including the pointer glow, which is motion whether or not anything is keyframed. The one exception is the scroll reveal, which switches to a **fade-only** keyframe rather than being disabled — the page still composes itself instead of snapping, without anything sliding across the screen.
- **The page is a long scroll on purpose** (~15 phone screens, in the shape of analystbuilder.com), and every section answers the next question a sceptic asks, in order: solve one now → chart your league (`chart-your-league.tsx`) → this week's real scores (`live-week-strip.tsx`, hides when nflverse is unreachable; **score tiles carry team crests via `components/team-logo.tsx` and performer rows carry headshots via `components/headshot.tsx`** — decided 2026-09-30, before which the product was colours-only because team marks are trademarks. ## Teaching voice (Duolingo-shaped)

Lesson copy lives mostly in `lib/curriculum.ts` (briefs, film cards, intros, prompts, explains). Write like a friendly coach — short, human, easy — not a textbook.

- **Short sentences.** One idea per beat. Prefer two short lines over one long clause stack.
- **Picture first, term second.** Show the spreadsheet / box score, *then* name “table” or “grain.” Never open a beat with a definition.
- **Talk to “you.”** Contractions are fine (`you're`, `don't`, `that's`). Skip passive lecture voice.
- **Football is seasoning, not a quiz.** Use sport flavor for energy; don’t require knowing play calls to understand SQL.
- **Cut filler and hype.** No “the sentence that unlocks everything,” “Do not let the word put you off,” or “That’s the whole deal.”
- **Film cards:** plain-English title + ≤2 short sentences + optional code. Brief notes = one gentle aside.
- **Explains:** one clear sentence after a miss. Prompts stay situation-based (not vocabulary lookups) — already the rule; keep them light.

## Projects (`/projects`)

One catalogue, two kinds of thing, split by how long they take and what you leave holding.

- **Builds** take an afternoon and end in a repo or notebook with your name on it. `lib/projects.ts`. The bar for adding one: it has to be something a learner could not have done on any other SQL site.
  - **Your League Scorecard** — pull your own fantasy league through the Sleeper API (or an ESPN/Yahoo CSV) in Colab and answer the questions managers argue about. Notebook: `notebooks/my-league-scorecard.ipynb`; rebuild it with `python scripts/build-league-scorecard-notebook.py`.
  - **Build the Warehouse** — a real dbt project in `dbt/nflverse_warehouse/`: raw nflverse release CSVs → staging views → `dim_players` + `fct_player_weeks`, with tests. **It runs on DuckDB deliberately** — no cloud account, no card, and `read_csv_auto` reads the releases straight off a URL, so the only barrier is `pip install dbt-duckdb`. The dbt is the same dbt you would run against Snowflake; only the profile changes.
  - `fct_player_weeks` joins the schedule on `game_id`, which the weekly file carries. The `unique` test on `player_week_key` is the safety net that the project is partly *about*: a join that fans out doubles every total downstream and errors nowhere.
  - **Build a Fantasy Prediction Model** — `notebooks/fantasy-points-model.ipynb`, generated by `python scripts/build-prediction-model-notebook.py`. Loads nflverse weekly stats since 2021, builds a last-3-games baseline, trains a gradient-boosted model on pre-kickoff features only (every feature is `shift(1)`-ed), tests on the latest complete season, breaks error down by position, leaks a feature on purpose to show what a lying score looks like, and projects the upcoming week from the real schedule. **`node scripts/test-prediction-model-notebook.mjs` runs every cell in Pyodide against the real files and fails if a cell errors or the model loses to the baseline** — first run: 4.92 vs 5.26 MAE on 2025 (−6.5%), better at every position.
- **Cases** take half an hour and end in a right answer — `lib/interview-cases.ts`, a brief, a schema you have never seen, three questions and a live terminal. These were "interview cases"; calling them interview prep undersold them. They ship their own tiny seed rather than using the lesson DB, so their answer keys stay stable. Progress is local (`sqlsports.interview.v1`) and never spends timeouts.
- A conversational AI interviewer remains future work.

## Gamified learning MVP (`/learn`)

Duolingo-style lesson player + DataCamp-style course roadmap, fully client-side (no accounts, no backend):

- **Catalog:** `/learn` (labelled **Courses** in the nav) is a card grid of all nine courses (`lib/courses.ts`); clicking one opens `/learn/track/[moduleId]`, which renders `components/course-roadmap.tsx`. The static `track` segment sits above the lesson player's `[lessonId]`, so the two don't collide. Courses with no lessons yet have `moduleId: null` and render as non-clickable "In build" cards — don't make one clickable before its lessons exist. Full lesson-by-lesson plans for all nine courses are in [docs/COURSE-CATALOG.md](docs/COURSE-CATALOG.md).
- **Content:** `lib/curriculum.ts` — 17 live units / 59 lessons / 271 exercises (mc, tap-the-word fill, write-real-SQL `query`, live `code` for Python/R, live `formula` for Excel). `u1`–`u6` are the complete SQL course; `u7`+`u13`+`u14` are the Python course (foundations → logic/functions → pandas for real data); `u8`–`u11` are statistics, visualization, Git/GitHub and R; `u15`–`u18` are the Excel course (grid/first formulas → logic → lookups → cleaning).
- **Unit ids are not display order.** `MODULES[].unitIds` defines both which units a course contains AND the order they appear in — `moduleUnits()` sorts by that list, not by position in `COURSE.units`, so units added later (Python's `u13`/`u14`) still sit beside `u7`. The roadmap numbers units by position within the selected module, so a course always reads Unit 1, 2, 3 regardless of its ids. Learners pick a course from the `/learn` catalog; the choice is stored via `lib/use-module.ts` so the lesson player advances within that course.
- **Live coding:** `query` exercises execute against sql.js (SQL only). `code` exercises run Python (Pyodide), R (WebR), or SQL via `lib/runtimes.ts` and grade printed output. `formula` exercises evaluate real Excel formulas against the workbook in `lib/excel-data.ts` (see below). Stats/viz/Git stay on `mc`/`fill` (no REPL). **Don't add a `query` exercise to units 7–11** — use `code` with `lang: "python" | "r"` when the learner should run real code, and **`formula` (never `query` or `code`) in the Excel units 15–18**.
- **Editors are `components/code-editor.tsx`** — every code surface (both lesson-player editors, the landing sandbox, the Practice Field) goes through it. It's a real `<textarea>` with a highlighted `<pre>` behind it and transparent text on top, so selection, undo, IME, mobile keyboards and screen readers all keep working; no CodeMirror/Monaco. Tokenizing lives in `lib/highlight.ts` (regex, not a parser — it only has to be stable per keystroke, and it returns token objects rather than HTML so learner input can never become markup). **The two layers must lay out glyphs identically or the caret drifts off the text** — shared geometry is in the `.code-layer` class and the padding/size classes are passed to both; change one, change the other. Syntax colours are `--c-syn-*` tokens in `globals.css` with a light-mode set, and all six clear AA in both themes.
- **Grading:** query exercises run learner SQL and the `expected` answer key against the same in-browser sql.js database (seeded from `lib/fantasy-data.ts`) and compare result values; `orderMatters: false` sorts rows first.
- **Excel runs for real** — `lib/excel-engine.ts` evaluates learner formulas against the workbook in `lib/excel-data.ts` (`Roster` clean, `Import` deliberately dirty, plus a blank `Practice` tab for free play) and grades on the **value produced**, not the formula text, so any correct spelling passes. Three things to know before touching it:
 - **The engine is `fast-formula-parser` (MIT) and the licence is the reason.** HyperFormula is more complete but **GPL-3.0-only**, which would force this whole app to GPL — never swap it in. The tradeoff is a partial function library, so `MAX`, `MIN`, `COUNTA`, `COUNTBLANK`, `MEDIAN`, `LARGE`, `SMALL`, `SUMIFS`, `COUNTIFS`, `AVERAGEIFS`, `MATCH`, `XLOOKUP`, `UPPER`, `LOWER`, `PROPER`, `TEXTJOIN`, `SUBSTITUTE` and `VALUE` are implemented in `customFunctions()`. Check a function actually evaluates before writing a lesson around it.
 - **A formula that references no cell is rejected even when its value is right** (`referencesCells()`), or a learner could read 402.5 off the grid and type `=402.5`. Set `allowLiteral: true` only if a drill genuinely has no cell to point at.
 - **`components/excel-grid.tsx` is read-only by design for Roster/Import.** Building an editable fake Excel on those tabs would teach habits that don't transfer; the learner reads real addresses off the grid and types real formulas against them. In lessons, clicking a cell inserts its A1 address into the formula bar. The free-play **Spreadsheet** at `/excel` adds Excel chrome (formula bar, sheet tabs, Enter-to-commit) on a writable Practice sheet — same engine, no grades, drills in `lib/excel-drills.ts`.
- **Run `node scripts/verify-answer-keys.mjs` after touching any `query`, Python `code`, or Excel `formula` exercise, or any `previewSql` / `previewSheet`.** Excel keys are evaluated by the shipped engine (loaded through `scripts/load-ts.mjs`, which transpiles project TS for Node) and fail on a key that errors, returns blank, names a missing sheet, or hardcodes its answer — that last one would mean the exercise's own anti-hardcode guard rejects the model answer. It also executes every Python answer key in Pyodide (same runtime as the browser) and fails if one errors or prints nothing — grading compares printed output, so a silent key marks every learner wrong. R keys are skipped (no supported Node build of WebR) and the script says so rather than passing them silently. It executes every answer key and brief preview against the real seeded database and fails on three things: a key that errors, a key returning zero rows, and a LIMIT whose cutoff is tied on *every* ORDER BY column (which lets a correct learner query grade wrong — add a tie-break to the prompt and the key). It already caught a brief previewing `fantasy_pts > 30` back when the dataset was synthetic and capped at 28.9.
- **The lesson data is REAL, and that is load-bearing.** `week_results` is nflverse-data weekly PPR fantasy points, regular season, one row per game actually played, **from 2022 through the newest completed week at build time** — rebuilt 2026-09-30 to run through week 3 of 2026: 1,221 rows, 20 players. It is baked into `lib/lesson-data.generated.ts` by `scripts/build-lesson-dataset.mjs` (re-run by hand; it also rewrites the learner CSVs in `public/data/`). Pinned still means pinned: a rebuild is a deliberate act, followed by the verifier. Things to keep true:
  - **Prose never types a number the data owns.** The builder also writes `lib/lesson-facts.generated.ts` — row count, teams, player-team pairs, McCaffrey's games against the games possible, the biggest game and everyone who shares it, the league's leader, the undrafted, the wire's top riser, per-season averages — and lesson copy, questions, the FAQ, `/data` and the front page interpolate `FACTS` instead. It is a separate small file so importing a fact never drags the dataset into a bundle. **`verify-answer-keys.mjs` re-derives every fact with SQL** and fails on any disagreement; it caught a real one on its first run (McCaffrey's four 2024 games average 11.95, which SQLite rounds to 11.9 and `Math.round` made 12.0 — averages are now summed in whole tenths and rounded with `toFixed`). Before this, the 2026 rebuild found prose claiming "nothing here clears 30" beside a 55.4, and "six players on nobody's roster" where there were ten.
  - **The newest season is partial on purpose.** The user asked for data current through 2026, so the pinned DB includes the in-progress season up to its last completed week. Aggregates with no season filter include it; prose that walks season by season says when the last number is a season still being played.
  - **Never go back to generated data.** The previous synthetic generator produced impossible history — Tyreek Hill on Miami in 2016, Puka Nacua playing before he was drafted — and a learner spotted it. `SELECT * FROM week_results LIMIT 5` also showed the same player five times, because rows were emitted player-major. The generated file is ordered season → week → points desc precisely so the first table anyone sees is five different players.
  - **`rosters` and `waiver_wire` come from Sleeper** (decided 2026-09-30; they were hand-invented before). The league plays **2024**, because every roster lesson scores it on 2024 games — a league drafted in 2026 would be scored on a season it never played. `waiver_wire` is **Sleeper's real 2024 wire going into week 10**: players under 50% rostered with the biggest week-over-week moves in rostered share (six risers, two fallers), `pct_rostered` = share of Sleeper leagues rostering him that week, `trend` = points that share moved since week 9, team as of that week from nflverse. `rosters` is **a five-team, two-round snake draft of the twenty-player lesson cast run on Sleeper's real 2024 PPR ADP** — the draft order is real, the five managers and their team names are ours, because a real league's rosters belong to the people in it (never pull a stranger's league). Only the cast is on the board because roster lessons join to `week_results`. Waiver players are *not* in the cast, so nothing may join `waiver_wire` to `week_results` expecting rows. `PROVENANCE` gives each table a `kind` (`real` / `drafted`) and a chip `label` (`real`, `real ADP`); `/data`, the schema panel and table peek render the label.
  - **Why Sleeper and not ESPN or Yahoo:** Sleeper's read-only API is free and needs no key. ESPN's equivalent is undocumented and its terms forbid automated access; Yahoo's needs an OAuth app and a user login. The ADP (`api.sleeper.com/projections`) and rostered-share (`api.sleeper.app/players/nfl/research`) endpoints are the ones Sleeper's own app calls and are not in its published docs — if a rebuild fails there, look there first. The Excel workbook's Owner and Salary columns are still an invented auction layer (separate owners, Jordan/Riley/Sam) and stay labelled as such.
  - **Attribution is required** (CC BY 4.0). `SHORT_CREDIT` from `lib/data-source.ts` appears on every lesson brief and in the sandbox schema panel, and `/data` carries the full citation plus free downloads. Don't remove them.
  - **Real data has gaps.** Missed games are absent, not zero — which is why `COUNT(*)` on McCaffrey returns `FACTS.gamesPlayed` rather than `FACTS.possibleGames`, and why the LEFT JOIN lessons have genuinely missing players (McCaffrey in 2024 weeks 1–2, among others). Lesson prose that quotes a number is checked against the database, not assumed; re-run the audit after any dataset rebuild.
- **The game layer (`lib/drive-sim.ts` + `lib/gameplay.ts` + `lib/achievements.ts`).** A lesson is one football possession. Every exercise is a play.
  - **Downs replace hearts.** Start 1st & 10 at the own 25. Correct → gain yards (`BASE_YARDS` + combo); if gain covers distance → new 1st & 10; if `ballOn >= 100` → Touchdown. Hard miss → incomplete or sack (burns a down); soft errors (syntax) cost nothing. 4th-down miss → Turnover on downs (fail screen). Misses **show the correct answer + why**, offer **Try again now** (free), and **re-queue ~2 snaps later** (Duolingo review, capped) via `lib/miss-feedback.ts`. Instant Replay / Challenge Flag still spend tickets to undo the down.
  - **Coach Assist** (`components/coach-assist.tsx` → `POST /api/coach`) can add a short tip on hint/miss when AI Gateway is configured; otherwise the written explain stays the source of truth.
  - **Yardage is not XP, deliberately.** XP measures effort and persists; yardage is physical LOS and is what animates. `BASE_YARDS` pays more for a `query`/`code`/`formula` drill (13) than for `mc` (6). A combo adds +1/play past the second, capped at +6. Perfect drive = zero downs burned.
  - **Play-call text is picked deterministically from the exercise index, never `Math.random()`** — random would re-roll the headline on every re-render and flicker while the learner reads it.
  - **`components/drive-field.tsx` draws real LOS.** Ball at `ballOn`, gold first-down stick at `firstDownAt`, scorebug (`1st & 10 · Ball on Own 25`), burst chips for `+N YDS` / `INCOMPLETE` / `SACK`. Physics live in `lib/drive-sim.ts`; booth voice (calls, heat, base yards) stays in `lib/gameplay.ts`.
  - **Badges are recomputed from stored stats, never incremented.** `statsFrom(progress)` → `earnedBadgeIds()`. That means a badge can't be lost to a failed write or duplicated across devices, and `progress.badges` exists purely to remember which unlocks were already *celebrated*. The trophy case on `/learn` shows locked badges with progress bars — an empty shelf gives a learner nothing to aim at.
  - Animations live in `globals.css` (`.animate-yard-pop` etc.) and are **switched off entirely under `prefers-reduced-motion`**, not just slowed.
  - **Lesson UX is DataCamp/Duolingo-shaped:** one beat per screen (paced brief → theory cards → one prompt + hands-on), sticky Check / Continue bar, immediate feedback, then the next play.
  - **The action bar is pinned at every width** (`.lesson-actionbar`, sticky bottom — brief, intro and plays). It used to be pinned only on phones; on a laptop it sat under the content, so grading a play grew a feedback panel above it and pushed Continue below the fold. After grading, the player measures the space between the scorebug and the bar and scrolls the feedback into it (all of it if it fits, else its top under the scorebug) — measured, because the bar is twice as tall after a miss. On phones the two recovery buttons share a row. The unit banner shows on the brief only; during plays the scorebug already names the lesson and the banner's height was what pushed things off screen.
  - **Arrow keys drive a lesson:** → is the screen's primary button (next beat, next card, Check, Continue, next lesson), ← steps back through the brief and intro, ↑/↓ move the multiple-choice selection. Keys typed into an editor or any text field are never intercepted. One window listener calls whatever handler the latest render left in `keyRef`, so it always sees current state; each primary button carries `aria-keyshortcuts` and a desktop-only → hint.
- **Progress:** localStorage only (`lib/progress.ts`, key `sqlsports.progress.v1`) — XP, daily streak, completed lessons, plus `badges` / `bestCombo` / `perfectLessons` / `totalYards`, plus the sideline economy (`timeouts`, `tickets`, `byeWeeks`, `seasonPass` — see `lib/economy.ts`). Every field is defaulted on load, so progress saved before a field existed still parses instead of resetting someone's XP. Use `EMPTY_PROGRESS` for initial state rather than hand-writing the literal. Swaps for the Supabase `progress` table when accounts land (LAUNCH-PLAN Phase 1).
- **Sideline economy (Free vs Season Pass):** Timeouts (5/day graded starts), scouting tickets (gems), bye weeks (streak freezes). Shop + waitlist on the learn rail; out-of-timeouts gate in the lesson player. Practice Field never spends a timeout. Real Stripe for Season Pass is still waitlist-only.
- **Beta bench (`/demo`):** internal QA surface, deliberately **not linked from the site nav**. Arms a demo profile in one click and links straight into every lesson with its exercise-type counts. It reads and writes the same localStorage progress the real player uses, so what you test is the real thing — keep it that way rather than giving it a mock mode.
- **Mascot: Coach Blitz** (`components/coach.tsx`) — a football with a coach's temper: huge eyes, heavy brows, eye black, white gloves, cleats, a whistle on a turf-green lanyard. Inline SVG, no image assets. Seven moods (`idle`, `happy`, `cheer`, `sad`, `think`, `angry`, `whistle`), each with its own motion — blink and glance, wave, squash-and-stretch jump, crossed arms with a tapping cleat. The API is still `mood` / `size` / `className`, so the redesign reached every call site without edits; pass `animated={false}` for a still pose.
  - **He is a football on purpose, not a bird.** He was redesigned to match a Duolingo-style mascot, and a round green bird would read as a copy of a trademarked character. Don't drift him toward Duo.
  - **Motion lives in `components/cast.module.css`**, not `globals.css`, so names like `blink` and `jump` can't collide. Animated SVG groups never carry a `transform` attribute (CSS transform would replace it) — nest instead. Pivots on joints use `transform-box: view-box` with origins in viewBox units; parts that scale about themselves use `fill-box`. All of it is off under reduced motion.
- **The path has a cast, and Coach walks it** (`components/path-cast.tsx`). One absolutely-positioned layer inside each `.path-board` (which must be `relative`) measures the board's pieces and stands characters beside them: Coach beside the current play, a Rookie (`components/sideline-cast.tsx`, tossing a ball) beside every chest, a Ref (signalling touchdown) at every course-clear and end zone. Boards opt in by tagging pieces: `data-path-node="<lessonId>"` on each node wrapper, `data-path-decor="chest" | "clear" | "endzone"` on the rest. `career-board.tsx` and `course-roadmap.tsx` both do.
  - **Coach hops node by node from where he last stood** (remembered per board under `sqlsports.coach.<boardKey>`) to your current play when you return having advanced, cheering; first visit he drops in. It's an overlay rather than something drawn inside the nodes because nested in a node he couldn't travel between them.
  - **A current node only counts after it holds still for 180ms.** Boards render once with empty progress before the real progress loads, which briefly makes lesson one look current; acting on that would replay a hop from lesson one on every visit.
  - **Positions are written as the base transform before any animation starts**, so a hop that never plays (hidden tab, reduced motion) leaves him at the right node rather than the old one.
  - **His mood follows the streak, which is where the personality is.** Played today → happy. Active yesterday but not today → **angry**, arms folded, with a gold bubble that stays up until you play (every other line fades after ~4s). No progress → whistle, "First snap's yours, rookie." Tapping him cycles lines; tapping him while angry gets you the whistle. Lines are picked by counter, never `Math.random`.
  - **The bubble opens away from the node** so it never covers the SNAP pill, flipping only if that side has under 120px — and its width is capped to the room left on the board, so it can't push a phone into sideways scroll.
- **Don't tilt a whole path stage in 3D.** `.path-board-stage` briefly carried `rotateX(7deg)` about its top edge. A career board's stage is ~9,000px tall, so its far end swung toward the camera by roughly the full perspective distance: nodes drew at 1.8× by node 20, 6× by node 40, 23× at the last, and the page scrolled thousands of pixels sideways. At the top, where it looked fine, it scaled by 1.03×. A tilt only works on a board of fixed height; give pieces their depth individually.
- **Beginner on-ramps use `brief.steps`.** A `LessonBrief` can carry a `steps` array (`BriefStep`: title / body / optional `code` / optional `note`) and the player paces them one beat at a time, holding back the preview table and the start button until the last one. Lessons without `steps` fall back to the single `setup` paragraph unchanged, so this is additive. **Use steps wherever a learner meets an idea for the first time** — `u1-l1` walks in over five beats (you are the league analyst → records are stat sheets → our league keeps three sheets → one row is one player-game → SELECT means show me) because the old single paragraph opened with "A database is a set of tables", which is a definition rather than an on-ramp. `u1-l2`, `u1-l3` and `u2-l1` have shorter walk-ins.
- **Every live lesson has a walk-in: 74 of 74, 156 beats.** A new lesson without `steps` is now the exception rather than the norm, so add them when you author one. When adding steps to a lesson, read what it actually teaches first — the beats should name the specific idea (LIMIT does not pick the best rows, it stops early) rather than restate the goal.
- **Questions test the concept, never our schema.** The sport is the lens; the thing being examined has to transfer to any database the learner ever opens. A question whose answer is `week_results` tests whether they memorised our invented table names, which is worth nothing to them. One got shipped and had to be replaced — it now asks what the first thing to work out about an unfamiliar table is, and the answer is what one row represents.
  - **Avoid bare vocabulary lookups** (which clause sorts? which function counts?). Put the learner in a situation instead: the leaderboard is showing the worst players at the top, the same query returned rows in a different order, you grouped by player and also selected week. Same knowledge, and it now tests whether they would recognise the problem in the wild.
  - **Check any factual premise against the real database before shipping it.** A reframed COUNT question claimed `COUNT(*)` and `COUNT(fantasy_pts)` disagree on `week_results`. They do not — it has no NULLs and both return the full row count — so the question was wrong in exactly the way a curious learner would discover.
- **If a beat names something, show it.** `BriefStep` takes `previewSql` / `previewSheet` / `previewCaption`, so the beat that says our league keeps three sheets renders `rosters` underneath itself, and the Excel cleaning unit shows the dirty Import tab while describing it. Naming a table without showing it asks the learner to picture something they have never seen. The lesson-level preview still appears on the final beat when a beat does not override it.
- **`components/schema-reference.tsx` sits under the SQL editor in every query drill.** Prompts name tables and columns constantly and there was previously nowhere to look them up mid-drive short of quitting the lesson. Collapsed by default, carries the real/example labels from `PROVENANCE`, and links to `/data`.
- **Say what is real when you introduce the tables.** The `u1-l1` walk-in states plainly that `week_results` is real NFL scoring, that `waiver_wire` is Sleeper's real wire, and that `rosters` is a draft on real ADP whose five managers are ours. A learner who assumes more than that has been misled by us, not by the data.
- **Every lesson opens with a `brief`** (`LessonBrief` in `lib/curriculum.ts`): `goal`, `setup`, and for SQL lessons a `previewSql` that runs live against the seeded database so the learner *sees the actual rows* before being asked anything about them. Adding a lesson without a brief is a regression; the field is required by the type. Hands-on drills hide hints behind **Need a hint?** until clicked.
- **XP is echoed in the learner's sport** — "+10 XP · 10 yards" for football, points/bases for basketball/baseball, driven by `lib/use-sport.ts`.
- **Practice Field (`/field`):** ungraded free-play sandbox over real NFL data. `scripts/build-field-dataset.mjs` downloads nflverse `stats_player` weekly CSVs (note: the *new* release tag — the older `player_stats` tag used by `lib/data/nflverse.ts` stops at 2024) and bakes `public/field-data.json` (~300 KB: latest-season weekly + 3-season summaries + 32 teams). Re-run it each season with `WEEKLY_SEASON` bumped. It is on **2026**, the season in progress, with 2024–2026 rollups; a partial season is held to the same scoring *pace* as the full-season 50-PPR bar, which otherwise kept forty players and emptied the weekly table. `lib/field-data.ts` holds the schema/seed builder and the drill-book prompts; UI in `components/field-sandbox.tsx`.
- **Rapid Fire (`/learn/rapid`):** timed multiple-choice from the live curriculum — pick SQL / Python / Excel / R / Stats / Git, 12s a question, earn scouting tickets. Zero timeouts. It is the fast cousin of the question bank: recall, not writing SQL.
- `middleware.ts` deliberately no-ops when `NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_URL` isn't a real http(s) URL (placeholder env values) — same lazy-init contract as `lib/stripe.ts`. Don't revert; with placeholder keys the Supabase client throws in middleware and takes down every route locally.

Don't add a checkout button before those land.
- **Email capture** — `POST /api/waitlist` → `public.waitlist` (migration 0003). Rows carry `interest`, `sport` and `source` so the list is segmented from day one. RLS allows INSERT only: **there is deliberately no SELECT policy**, so the anon key can't read the list back. Read it from the dashboard or with the service role.
- **Accounts** — magic link only, at `/account`, with `/auth/callback` exchanging the code. Accounts are **optional**: everything works signed out, and progress stays in localStorage until someone signs in. `lib/progress-sync.ts` merges local and remote by **unioning completed lessons and taking max of counters** — last-write-wins would delete work when a learner switches devices. Progress lives in `public.learner_progress` (migration 0004), which mirrors the `Progress` type 1:1; the older `lesson_completions` table from 0001 is shaped for the retired 12-week syllabus and is unused.
- **`middleware.ts` skips anonymous visitors** (no `sb-*-auth-token` cookie → return immediately) and races `getUser()` against a 2.5s timeout. Both matter: it otherwise made a blocking network call on every page view for visitors with no session, and a slow auth server would hang the whole site instead of degrading to signed-out.

## Deployment

- Vercel project under `jomckinney1999s-projects`, linked via `.vercel/` (gitignored; its `projectName` still reads `sql-sports`). Production is **https://data-draft.vercel.app** since 2026-09-30; the old `sql-sports.vercel.app` redirects there, so verify deploys against the new host — curl on the old one only ever sees a 307.
- **The GitHub repo is `jomckinney1999/datadraft` and it is public on purpose** (renamed from `SQL-Sports`, opened 2026-09-30): Colab's "Open in Colab" only opens notebooks from a public repo. The notebooks are also served from the site (`public/notebooks/`, written by the builder scripts) with a Download button, so the builds never depend on GitHub alone.
- Production branch is `main` (not `master` — `master` is a stale GitHub default branch with no app code, left over from initial repo setup).
- Framework preset on Vercel is explicitly set to `nextjs` — don't let this get unset, it previously caused production to silently serve only `/public` with everything else 404ing.
- **Pushing to `main` deploys production on its own**, through Vercel's Git integration. Observed 2026-09-21: the CLI deploy was rejected at authentication and created nothing, yet the pushed commit was live within about a minute. So the normal flow is just `git push origin main`, then **confirm on the live site** — fetch a page and look for something only the new commit contains, rather than assuming the build went green.
- The CLI is the fallback, for deploying without a push or re-deploying after an env-var change: `npx vercel deploy --prod --scope jomckinney1999s-projects`. The `--scope` flag is required — without it the CLI fails with a misleading `Not authorized` even when `npx vercel whoami` shows you logged in. **Its saved login expires**, and when it does the CLI answers `reason: "not_authorized"` / "A new login is required" (JSON, not the scope error above). The fix is `npx vercel login` in an interactive terminal, which a Claude session cannot complete — ask the user rather than retrying.
